## The Observation

I run. Not competitively—just a few times a week to clear my head. I had a running app that tracked my pace, distance, elevation. All useful data. But it didn't feel alive.

The numbers told me I ran 5.2 kilometers. But they didn't tell me *why* that run mattered. They didn't capture the morning I woke up anxious and came back calm. They didn't tell the story.

Meanwhile, I noticed running communities around me—small groups who'd meet at dawn, push each other, celebrate each other's milestones. But they had no good way to share the *why* behind their runs. Just Strava splits and Garmin files.

## The Question

What if running data could be woven into narrative? What if you could combine your numbers with your story?

## Building RunnersPod

RunnersPod started as a thought experiment: **A place to document runs not just as data, but as moments.**

The app captures:
- Traditional metrics: pace, distance, time, elevation, heart rate
- **But also:** mood before and after, what you noticed, who you ran with, how it felt
- Community features: share runs with your running crew, celebrate milestones together
- Storytelling: over time, your runs become a log of who you are, not just what you did

I built it because I wanted to:
- **Respect the act of running.** It's not just exercise—it's meditation, therapy, community, challenge.
- **Make data personal.** Stats alone are hollow. A run is a story.
- **Build for community.** Whether you run solo or with a group, there's a place for your voice.
- **Keep it simple.** No social theater. No vanity metrics. Just honest running.

## The Joy

The best part? Watching runners discover something they didn't expect: their runs become a journal. A year of entries. A story of resilience.

One runner told me: *"I was going through a breakup. I re-read my run logs from that month. And I could see exactly when I started getting better. My runs don't lie."*

That's RunnersPod. It's not just a running app. It's a way to tell your story—in miles, minutes, and meaning.

## The Problem

My grandmother takes eight medications a day. Every morning, she'd stand at the kitchen counter with a handful of bottles, trying to remember which ones she'd already taken. Sometimes she'd take the same pill twice. Sometimes she'd skip one entirely.

She's not alone. Medication non-compliance affects millions—leading to hospitalizations, worsening conditions, and unnecessary suffering.

I watched her struggle, and I thought: *There has to be a better way.*

## The Wrong Solutions

I looked at what was available. Most medication reminder apps were either:
- **Bloated**: Packed with health tracking features nobody asked for
- **Expensive**: Charging subscriptions for basic reminders
- **Intrusive**: Spamming notifications like spam alerts
- **Privacy-invasive**: Collecting data they didn't need

None of them felt built with care for the user. They felt built for the business model.

## Building MedTrack

So I started fresh. I wanted to build an app that:
- **Works offline.** No internet? Your medications are still there.
- **Costs nothing.** Ever. No ads, no in-app purchases, no subscriptions.
- **Respects privacy.** All your data stays on your phone. Period.
- **Does one job well.** Track medications. Set reminders. That's it.

The technical side was straightforward—local storage, simple UI, push notifications. But the real work was in the design. Making it intuitive for people with no tech literacy. Making it fast even on older phones. Making every interaction respect the user's time.

## What It Means

When my grandmother started using MedTrack, she forgot a pill exactly once. Then she caught herself. The system was there, reminding her gently, without judgment.

That's the goal. Not to add complexity to someone's life, but to remove the burden they already carry.

Every medication taken on time. Every dose tracked. No stress.
